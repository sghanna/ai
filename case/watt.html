Career case study | Wells Fargo | Product owner, 2009 to 2015

Wholesale Analyst Tracking Tool Built around the people who used it.

For six years I was the product owner of the internal system Wells Fargo used to track analysts through its Wholesale Banking credit training programs. I inherited it as an Access database, turned what its users told me into a roadmap of planned releases, and gave the IT team requirements they could build within budget. Then I trained the users and wrote the procedures so the team could run it without me.

01Problem

Tracking that could not keep up

The tool tracked each analyst through the credit training curriculum. Before it was modernized, a web tool and fragmented Access-based processes made tracking training progress slow, manual and hard to scale.

  • I started as the database's keeper. In my first Wells Fargo role I inherited the Access database that became the tool, and kept it running with queries, forms and reports.
  • Reporting was manual. Regional program managers prepared progress reports by hand, and each analyst's progress sat in several HR and learning systems.
  • The programs were growing. The early talent programs the tool supported grew from 200 analysts to more than 1,000 participants and 700 managers.

Making the case. I built the case for change with stakeholders over time, then moved the tool and its processes to the web with the IT team. Along the way I moved from database maintainer to product owner.

02Research and prototyping

Starting with the people using it

I applied Design Thinking to the tool. Adoption rose from 24% to 89%.

  • Research. Personas, journey maps and empathy interviews, alongside interviews with managers and the needs of the program directors.
  • Requirements. I wrote the tool's requirements from the program directors' needs and the interviews with managers.
  • Prototypes. I led lo-fi and hi-fi prototyping and continuous user testing, and iterative Agile development improved the tool's usability.

The manager dashboard

One place to see each analyst's progress

We updated the tool to give managers direct access to the progress of each analyst they managed, drawn from various HR and learning systems, in a single dashboard. Making the reports a direct-access dashboard cut regional program managers' manual reporting time by 90%.

  • A full picture. Each analyst's record covered recruiting, course completions and scores, and manager evaluations. The tool also captured scores from a live Loan Committee and produced a printable report for each analyst and their manager at the end of the week-long in-person course in San Francisco.
  • Access that kept itself current. I directed a nightly process that used the who-reports-to-whom data in the corporate directory feed to add and remove manager access automatically.
  • Room for real reporting lines. A manager could delegate dashboard access to a team lead whose dotted-line role the corporate directory did not capture.

03Roadmap and backlog

Deciding what came next

I owned the roadmap of planned releases, and I owned and prioritized the issue backlog the IT team worked through for my whole time in the role, 2009 to 2015.

  • Feasible within budget. I worked with IT to keep requirements feasible within budget and focused on what the program needed.
  • A rebuild with a purpose. Over 80% of the tool was rebuilt under my direction, to make it easier to use, to handle more day-to-day reporting and tracking, and to clean up technical debt. The rebuild added features at the same time.
  • The whole lifecycle. The tool tracked the full participant lifecycle: interested students at diversity events, interns, full-time analysts, program graduates, Credit Management Training Program participants, alumni and hiring managers.

04IT's implementation

IT built it. I defined and tested it.

The tool was a home-grown system. The IT team did the building; my job was to give them clear requirements and to test what they delivered.

What I did

  • Wrote the requirements and oversaw requirements gathering and documentation.
  • Directed IT to build the functionality from the earlier Access database into the tool.
  • Created the procedure staff used to download course-completion data from the learning management system and upload it in a consistent format the tool could parse.
  • Wrote the test scripts, managed testing, and oversaw quality assurance and user acceptance testing.

What IT did

  • Built the functionality I directed into the tool, including what had lived in the earlier Access database.
  • Built the processing of the uploaded learning-system data, folding it into the analyst reporting and dashboard system I designed.

05Training and handoff

Built to run without me

  • Training. I delivered the training for the tool.
  • Procedures. I wrote the standard operating procedures, and kept them updated, so the team could continue running the tool without me.
  • Lasting use. The tool continued to work and was used extensively for the duration of the program.
