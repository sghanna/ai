A little help at the table.

Each turn

  1. Draw one card, or plan a pickup of the whole discard pile.
  2. Meld matching ranks. Select cards, press Stage selected, then Confirm melds. Stage several ranks together to reach your opening minimum. You can clear a draft before confirming.
  3. Discard one card. Select it, then press Discard selected. You can change your selection first.

Build a canasta

A meld has at least three cards, including two natural cards of the same rank. Twos and jokers are wild; each meld can have at most three wilds. Seven or more cards make a canasta. Your partner shares your melds.

A natural canasta earns 500 bonus points; a mixed one earns 300. Your team needs a canasta to go out. Until then, keep a card after discarding. You may go out by melding everything or discarding your last card.

Opening your team

The first melds must total 15 points if your score is negative, 50 below 1,500, 90 below 3,000, or 120 above that. The minimum stays visible by your score. Bonuses don't count toward it.

The discard pile

Plan pile pickup shows the cards you will meld before taking the rest. A frozen pile requires two matching natural cards from your hand. A wild card freezes the pile until it is taken. Before your team opens, the pile is frozen for you too.

An unfrozen pile can be taken onto your team's existing meld, with a matching pair, or with one matching natural and a wild. A wild or three on top blocks pickup. With a one-card hand you cannot take a one-card pile.

Threes

Red threes are put aside automatically and replaced when dealt or drawn. Red threes taken from the discard pile have no replacement. Each earns 100 points if your team melds; all four earn 800. Those points are subtracted if your team never melds.

Black threes block the next pickup but don't freeze the pile. Meld three or four black threes only when going out, without wilds.

Scoring and endings

Jokers are worth 50; aces and twos 20; 8 through king 10; 4 through 7 and black threes 5. Add melded card points and bonuses, then subtract cards left in both teammates' hands. Going out earns 100, or 200 when concealed: no earlier melds by that player, no adding to partner's melds, and a new canasta in the final turn.

A concealed exit from a stock draw can waive the opening minimum. With no stock left, taking an unfrozen discard that matches your team's meld is mandatory, unless the one-card restriction applies. Otherwise you may end the hand. Drawing a red three as the last stock card ends it immediately.

The team with the higher score wins after a completed hand reaches 5,000. A tie continues. Going out doesn't require asking your partner; this version has no partner-permission option.

This table follows classic rules, chiefly Pagat, with the one-card pickup restriction described by Bicycle. It is not Modern American Canasta. Computer players use simple strategies.
