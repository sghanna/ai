Wells Fargo | Technology enablement

Recruiting around the clock

Our recruiting system let only one person own and edit a requisition, so teams in the US and the Philippines could not share the work. I built an online tool that let them pass it back and forth: the team in the Philippines completed work such as resume screening overnight, and the US owner entered it the next morning.

It worked, and hiring demand was high: within 30 days the number of roles worked through the tool doubled.

03Approach

How I work

Step 1

Start with the person.

Watch the real problem: a card too small to read, a button too easy to miss, a report nobody uses. Turn it into a requirement the finished work can be checked against.

IN PRACTICEMy mother's games.

When her finger drifted off a card, slow presses and drift support became a requirement for every game that followed.

Step 2

Make the options visible.

Ask for working alternatives, compare them where they will be used, and keep the reason for the choice.

IN PRACTICEThree AI builders.

I gave Claude, Codex, and Antigravity the same kind of brief, compared their work at real screen sizes, and kept the strongest.

Step 3

Check before accepting.

A second AI reviews the first. Defects get reproduced and fixed. Humans are always in the loop and participate in testing that cannot be replicated by a machine.

IN PRACTICEThree open-source fixes.

Claude Code and Codex each checked the other's work. The maintainer merged all three, unchanged.
